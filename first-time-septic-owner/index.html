How does a septic system work?

Wastewater from toilets, sinks, showers, and laundry flows into an underground tank. Inside the tank, heavier solids settle in the inlet compartment as sludge. Lighter material, including grease, floats as scum. Wastewater in the middle, called effluent, moves into the outlet compartment and then out to the drain field.

If the tank has an effluent filter, it sits in the outlet side, under the lid, and helps keep solids from leaving the tank. From there, effluent soaks into the drain field. Bacteria in the soil, including a living layer called the biomat, treat it before it moves farther toward groundwater. For a fuller explanation, see why good bacteria protect your tank and drain field.

How often should I pump a septic tank?

The U.S. EPA SepticSmart guidance is that most household tanks are pumped every 3 to 5 years. That is a general range, not a fixed schedule for every home. A larger household, a smaller tank, a garbage disposal, or heavy water use can mean you need service sooner. Snowbird and rental homes can also fill faster when they are occupied. We do not guess your interval from a chart alone; household size and tank size are the starting point, and an inspection shows what your tank actually needs. See how often to pump your septic tank for more detail.

What should I do first after moving into a house with a septic system?

  1. Find the tank and lids. Lids can be buried under grass or soil. Locating and digging up a buried lid is $100, plus $50 for each additional hour. If you know where the lid is, mark it or tell us when you call.
  2. Ask for records. Previous pumping dates, permits, and inspection reports tell you whether the system is current or overdue.
  3. Schedule a pump-out, and an inspection if you do not know the system's condition. A septic inspection with pump-out is $549.99. That is a practical first step when records are missing, especially after a purchase.
  4. Ask about risers. A 6-inch riser ($299.99 each) brings the lid up to ground level so later visits do not start with digging.

How do I care for the effluent filter?

If your tank has an effluent filter, it should be checked and cleaned at every pump-out and whenever drains slow. There is no single calendar interval that fits every house. A dirty filter can cause backups or slow drains, and wastewater can be pushed back into the house. Express cleans your filter at no charge on pump-outs and can show you how to keep it clean. See effluent filter care.

What should never go down the drains or toilet?

  • Wipes, including ones labeled flushable
  • Cooking grease, fats, and oil
  • Bleach and harsh cleaners in large amounts, which can kill helpful bacteria
  • Medications
  • Paint and solvents
  • Feminine products, paper towels, and diapers

Septic-safe, biodegradable toilet paper is easier on the system than thick wipes. For habits that protect bacteria, see good bacteria and the drain field and our maintenance tips.

What are the drain field do's and don'ts?

  • Do not park, drive, or build on the drain field
  • Do not plant trees or large shrubs over it; grass is the better cover
  • Divert roof gutters and runoff away from the field
  • Do not drain a swimming pool or spa onto the field; a large volume of chlorinated water can overload it
  • A garbage disposal adds solids, so you may need pumping more often
  • A water softener can send extra water into the system when it recharges; factor that into how often you pump

If the field is already soggy or backing up, see what to do when a drain field is not draining. Replacement, if it is needed, is quoted after we inspect the system.

How should I use water in a septic home?

Spread laundry through the week instead of running many loads in one day. Fix leaky toilets and faucets. A sudden flood of water can push solids toward the drain field before they have time to settle in the tank.

What warning signs mean I should call?

  • Sewage odors inside or outside
  • Gurgling pipes
  • Slow drains
  • Wet spots or unusually lush grass over the drain field
  • Toilets or drains backing up
  • An alarm, if your system has one

What is different about septic systems in Southwest Florida?

Much of this area has sandy soil and a high water table, especially in the rainy season. That leaves less room between the drain field and groundwater, so a full tank or a saturated yard can show problems sooner. After a hurricane or flood, wait until standing water recedes before using the system, then have it checked. Snowbirds returning to a house that sat unused should confirm when the tank was last pumped before filling it with several days of heavy use. See hurricane and flood septic prep and seasonal and snowbird septic care.

What should I do first if I just moved into a house with a septic tank?

How often should a first-time owner pump the tank?

The U.S. EPA's SepticSmart guidance is that most household tanks are pumped every 3 to 5 years. Household size, tank size, and water use change that. See how often to pump.

Where is the effluent filter and how often should I clean it?

If your tank has one, it is in the outlet side, under the lid. Check and clean it at every pump-out and whenever drains slow. Express cleans the filter at no charge on pump-outs. See effluent filter care.

Wipes, even those labeled flushable, grease and oil, large amounts of bleach or harsh cleaners, medications, paint, and feminine products. Septic-safe, biodegradable toilet paper is a better choice than thick or 'flushable' wipes.
