Immokalee is an inland agricultural town in Collier County, and a drain field here is a remaining-tank story, not a coastal-lot story: many in-town addresses are billed by Immokalee Water & Sewer District, while rural houses and farms around town still treat wastewater on the lot. Check the bill before you look for laterals. This page is only for a property that still has a field. Call (239) 451-3261 or request an Immokalee visit on ServiceM8.

Why are farm-town kitchen leftovers a problem for a remaining field?

Oil, gravy, and fats should go in the trash, not down a sink in town or on a farm road. They gather as scum and can move toward the laterals on a system that already has no sewer to fall back on. We do not invent a local failure count for Immokalee fields. We do treat kitchen grease as a don't on any lot that still has one.

What belongs in the trash in an Immokalee bathroom?

Even wipes sold as flushable should leave an Immokalee bath in a bag. Choose a roll made to break down in a septic tank. Older baths and busy multi-generational houses are common places for wipes to pile up; the tank still has to handle whatever is flushed.

Harsh cleaners and the organisms an inland field still needs

Do not empty a leftover jug of disinfectant into an Immokalee drain. Those products can set back the treatment that happens in the tank and in the soil. Washing a bath the ordinary way is not the target. Being inland does not change that chemistry. See bacteria in the tank and the soil they feed.

Parking, shade trees, and runoff on in-town versus rural lots

An in-town yard can hide the field in a tight side lot. A farm or rural parcel can hide it far from the house, where trucks and equipment roll without thinking about laterals. In both cases, keep vehicles off the field, and keep pools, spas, and outbuildings off that footprint. Deep roots should stay clear; turf is enough cover. Send roof water and irrigation somewhere else, especially after a heavy summer rain when the ground is already soft.

Spreading laundry in a busy household, and staying on a pump-out

In a larger or multi-generational house, run the washers on different days so the field is not asked to take one long pulse. A disposal contributes solids; do not let the pump-out lag so those solids are not asked to travel into the field. Immokalee kitchens: confirm the district, do not invent a schedule

Immokalee Water & Sewer District lists a grease-control program, but we could not independently retrieve the district's published pumping intervals, so this page does not invent a 30-day or 90-day IWSD number. Confirm the current schedule with the district. If the kitchen is actually on Collier County sewer, the county's published 30-day indoor-trap and 90-day interceptor intervals apply. Immokalee drain field do's and don'ts questions

Is every Immokalee house on a drain field?

No. Many in-town addresses are served by Immokalee Water & Sewer District. This page is for a house that still uses a tank, including rural and farm properties around town. Check the bill. Call (239) 451-3261.

Can I use Collier County's 30/90-day grease numbers automatically?

Only if the kitchen actually discharges to Collier County sewer. Many Immokalee sites are on the district system; confirm that schedule with the district rather than inventing an IWSD number here.

Do farm or older houses get different flushing rules?

No. Wipes and leftover grease are a problem for any remaining field. A biodegradable, septic-safe roll is still the right paper. Access and lot size can differ; the don'ts do not.
