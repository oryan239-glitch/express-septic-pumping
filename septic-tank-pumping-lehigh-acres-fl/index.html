Septic in Lehigh Acres: most homes have a tank

Central water and sewer in Lehigh Acres is run by the Florida Governmental Utility Authority (FGUA), which reports about 10,420 wastewater customers here (FGUA, Lehigh Acres service area description). Lee County has counted 32,879 permitted septic tanks in Lehigh Acres (Lee County, Lehigh Acres septic-to-sewer planning document). So most Lehigh homes use a septic tank, often alongside a private well.

Not sure which you have? If you don't pay a sewer charge to FGUA, your home most likely has a septic tank.

Lee County notes that many Lehigh Acres homes were built from the 1960s through the early 1980s (Lee County, Lehigh Acres septic-to-sewer planning document). If you own an older home and don't know when the tank was last pumped, start with a pump-out so you know where things stand.

On a well-and-septic lot, the drain field and your well share the same ground. Pumping on schedule keeps solids out of the drain field. Lehigh is flat, and the summer rainy season raises the water table, so pumping before summer gives the system more room.

How often should I pump my septic tank?

The U.S. EPA recommends pumping most household tanks every 3 to 5 years. Tank size, household size and water use change that. Pumping before Florida's summer rainy season gives the drain field more room.

What is a septic tank riser?
