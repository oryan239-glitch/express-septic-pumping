Why the well and the filter belong in the same conversation

On a well-and-septic lot, the drain field and the well are in the same soil. Pumping on schedule keeps sludge in the tank. The filter is the last screen on the way out. If it is packed, wastewater can back up into the house. If it is missing or damaged, solids can move toward the field. We will not invent a well-contamination statistic. We will say that a clean filter is one of the few inexpensive steps that protects both the house plumbing and the field on a lot like this.

Older Lehigh tanks may not match a newer diagram

Lee County has noted that many Lehigh Acres homes were built from the 1960s through the early 1980s. Some of those tanks were never fitted with an effluent filter. Some were retrofitted. Some newer houses have a cartridge that is easy to miss if you have never opened the outlet lid. We will tell you what is actually there. If there is a filter, clean it when we pump, and clean it again if drains slow. That is the interval we will publish, not a monthly number. Lehigh is flat, and the summer rainy season raises the water table, so a clogged filter in June or July leaves even less margin. Lots along Lee Boulevard, Homestead Road, and the residential grid can hide lids in grass, gravel, or a back corner that has not been opened in years.
