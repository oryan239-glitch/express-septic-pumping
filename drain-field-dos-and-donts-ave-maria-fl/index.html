In Ave Maria, start by asking whether wastewater even leaves the lot through a tank: the planned town in eastern Collier County has its own utility company, and many addresses in the compact center, along Pope John Paul II Boulevard, and near Ave Maria University are on that sewer system. This page is only for a property that still treats wastewater in its own tank, including houses on the fringe of town. It is not a copy of the Golden Gate Estates habit page next door. Does every Ave Maria address even have a drain field?

No. A sewer charge on an Ave Maria Utility Company bill means there is no household field to protect, and the don'ts on this page do not apply. If a tank is still in service, cooking oil and fats should still be kept out of the sink. They cling in the line and can work toward the field once the house fills up during a busy campus week or a winter stay. This town page does not invent a failure rate.

What should not go in a toilet if the house still has a tank?

If it is a wipe, even one whose wrapper says flushable, it goes in the garbage in a house that still feeds a field. Choose paper made to break down in a septic tank. Guests who are used to sewered apartments in the town center may not know the difference; a short note in the bath helps more than a later backup.

Do campus weeks change the cleaning rules, or only the load?

University calendars and seasonal occupancy change how quickly the tank fills. They do not make leftover bleach or a harsh cleaner safe to dump. Those products can set back the microbes that treat waste before effluent reaches the soil. Ordinary cleaning of a town bath is not the target. See a short read on tank and soil bacteria.

Pavers, parking, and plantings when a field is still in service

Town-center hardscape and fringe-lot grass hide a field differently, but the rule is the same: leave cars and trucks off the laterals, and site a pool, a spa, or a building somewhere else. Deep-rooted plantings should stay clear of that ground. Turn roof drains and spray heads toward other ground so rain is not soaking soil that already has to accept effluent.

Laundry and disposals during a busy university week

When the house fills up, space washing through the week rather than running every machine on the same afternoon. A disposal adds solids on top of that load. Town kitchens and why we do not copy a county FOG calendar

Ave Maria Utility Company provides water and sewer for the town and is not Collier County Public Utilities. Ave Maria drain field do's and don'ts questions

How do I know if this Ave Maria page even applies?

Check the bill. Many addresses in the planned town center are on Ave Maria Utility Company sewer and would not have a household field. This page is only for a house that still uses a tank. Call (239) 451-3261.

Do university weeks change what we can flush?

No. Event weeks and winter occupancy change how fast a tank fills, not the don'ts. Wipes, even flushable labels, still belong in the trash. A biodegradable, septic-safe roll is still the right paper.

Can I copy Collier County's 30-day and 90-day grease intervals onto a town kitchen?

No. Those intervals belong to the county sewer FOG program. Ave Maria Utility Company is a different provider. Confirm AMUC's current grease schedule rather than inventing a town number here.
