Septic backup after heavy rain

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

If toilets or drains backed up during or right after a heavy rain, stop adding water and call (239) 451-3261. A storm can push a septic system that was already close to full, or a drain field that was already saturated, over the edge. This page is about that rain-related backup, not about every overflow and not about a hurricane. For any indoor sewage overflow, the immediate steps are the same as our emergency backup page: stop running water, keep people and pets away from spilled sewage, and do not use the online booking form while it is happening. After-hours pumping is $599.99.

Why would the house back up after a storm when it was fine yesterday?

A drain field needs unsaturated soil to take effluent. After a hard rain, especially in June through September, many Southwest Florida yards already have a high water table. If the tank is also due, or if an outlet filter is clogged, there is little room left when the field cannot accept as much water as it did on a dry day. The rain did not always destroy the field overnight. It often shows a system that had been getting by. A house that only acts up after storms, then seems fine in dry weeks, still needs a look at the tank rather than a shrug until the next squall.

What should I do during and right after the rain?

Stop laundry, dishwashing, and showers. Do not keep flushing. If the tank or drain field is under standing water, do not use the system and do not wade around lids or electrical equipment to inspect it yourself. When you can reach us safely, call and describe whether sewage is inside the house, whether the yard is flooded, and when the tank was last pumped if you know. A planned pump-out, once the overflow is under control and the visit is not after-hours emergency work, starts at $399.99 for a standard 1–3 bedroom home. If you are mid-overflow, call rather than requesting a routine time on ServiceM8.

Is this a one-time saturation problem or a sign the field is struggling?

One storm can overwhelm a field that usually works. Repeated backups after ordinary summer rain, wet spots that linger in the same footprint, or lush, smelly grass over the field are reasons to look further. We start with the tank: pump both compartments, clean the effluent filter at no charge if there is one, and tell you what we saw. A pump-out can relieve a backup caused by a full tank. It will not rebuild a field that has failed. Drain field replacement is quoted only after we inspect the system. For lingering soggy footprints, see wet spots and green grass and drain field not draining.

How is this different from a hurricane or a flooded yard?

A heavy afternoon rain and a named storm are not the same event. If the lot is under floodwater, storm surge, or standing water that will not recede, follow hurricane and flood septic prep: do not use the system while it is submerged, then have it checked when the lids are reachable. If you are thinking ahead to the wet months rather than standing in a backup right now, the rainy season guide is the better planning page. This page stays on the backup that shows up with a storm.

When should I schedule a pump-out versus call after hours?

Call after hours when sewage is overflowing or you cannot stop using a fixture that is already coming up. After-hours pumping is $599.99. If the rain has passed, the overflow has stopped, and you want the tank emptied before the next storm, a planned visit is appropriate. Mention access issues, buried lids ($100 to locate and dig, plus $50 for each additional hour), and whether an alarm sounded. We serve homes across Lee, Collier, and Charlotte County, plus LaBelle, including lots that sit wetter than average in the rainy season.

Backup after heavy rain questions

Why would a system that was fine yesterday back up after a storm?

A heavy rain can saturate the soil around a drain field, especially when the water table is already high. A tank that is due to be pumped has less room to spare. The storm did not always 'break' the field; it can reveal a system that was already close to its limit. Call (239) 451-3261.

Should I use the house while the yard is still underwater?

Do not run water into a system if the tank or drain field is under standing water. Wait until you can reach the lids safely, then have the system checked before you return to normal use.

Is a rain backup the same as hurricane damage?

Not necessarily. This page is about backups tied to heavy rain. For a named storm, storm surge, or a lot that stayed flooded, use hurricane and flood septic prep.

What does after-hours pumping cost if this happens at night?

After-hours pumping is $599.99. Call (239) 451-3261. Do not use the online booking form while sewage is overflowing.
