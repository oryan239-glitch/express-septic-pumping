What landscapers and pool builders should not plant or build over a drain field

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

A landscaping plan or a new pool can quietly ruin a septic drain field if the field is treated as leftover yard. This page is for landscapers, irrigation crews, and pool builders working in Southwest Florida. It is about what not to plant, pave, or set on the field, and how to find out where that field is before you bid. It is not a homeowner pool-draining guide; that is pools and septic systems. Job-site pumping and equipment traffic are also covered for general contractors on builders and contractors and construction sites. Call (239) 451-3261 if the owner needs the tank or lids located before your work starts.

What should never go on top of the drain field?

Do not park, drive, or stage equipment on it. Do not set a shed, a cabana, a fire pit, a berm, or a heavy decorative wall on it. Do not pour a deck, a spa pad, or a pool equipment slab on it. Do not pile fill or stockpile stone on it. The field needs unsaturated soil and intact laterals. Weight compacting that soil, and construction traffic during a pool or landscape project, can show up later as a soggy footprint or a backup in the house. If you do not know where the field is, find it before the first delivery, not after the pad is formed.

What should not be planted over the field?

Skip trees and large shrubs in the drain-field footprint. Roots look for moisture and can invade lines. Grass is the usual, appropriate cover. Do not plant a garden that needs constant tilling, and do not use the field as the place to hide a row of ficus or a hedge to screen a pool. Irrigation that soaks the field on a timer adds water the soil may not be able to take, especially in the rainy season. Divert roof downspouts and irrigation runoff away from the field rather than toward it. We are not giving a botanical species list as a scientific inventory; the practical rule is to keep woody roots and extra water off that footprint.

How do we locate the tank and field before we bid?

Ask the owner for as-builts, a prior pump-out sketch, or a survey. If lids are buried, the owner can have us locate and dig for $100, plus $50 for each additional hour; details are on can't find the septic tank lid. A riser at $299.99 each can be installed once the lid is open so later service does not start with a shovel through your new sod. Do not probe wildly with a mini excavator. If you uncover a lid or a wet, smelly area mid-job, stop in that footprint and get the owner to call us.

What about grade changes, retaining walls, and drainage work?

Raising or cutting grade over the field can bury lids, change how water moves, and smother a system that was working. A retaining wall or a swale that points stormwater at the field can saturate it. If your scope includes regrading around a pool cage or a new lanai, treat the field as a constraint in the plan, not as fill you can borrow. After a heavy rain, a stressed field is more obvious; that is a reason to leave it alone, not a reason to trench it. Homeowners reading wet spots can use wet spots and green grass.

When should the owner pump before your project starts?

If the tank is due, or records are missing, a pump-out before a long project is a good idea, especially if crews will be using the house. A planned residential pump-out starts at $399.99 (standard 1–3 bed). The owner can request a routine visit on ServiceM8 or call. If your work will close up the yard, schedule access first. Drain field replacement, if the field is already failed, is quoted after we inspect the system, not from a landscape sketch. We serve these coordination calls in Lee, Collier, and Charlotte County, plus LaBelle.

Landscapers and pool builders questions

Can we plant trees over a drain field to help it 'drink' the water?

No. Trees and large shrubs can send roots into the field and compact or shade it in unhelpful ways. Grass is the better cover. Call (239) 451-3261 if you need the field located before a planting plan.

Can a pool deck, a shed, or a patio sit on the drain field?

Do not build or set heavy structures on the field. Parking, pads, and decks can compact soil and damage pipes. Locate the tank and field before you bid the work.

Who should call if we uncover a lid or a wet area mid-job?

Stop work in that footprint and have the owner call (239) 451-3261, or call with the owner's permission. Do not trench through a suspected field to 'drain it.'

Do you install landscaping or build pools?

No. We pump septic tanks and can help you keep the system clear of the new work. Pool owners can also read pools and septic systems.
