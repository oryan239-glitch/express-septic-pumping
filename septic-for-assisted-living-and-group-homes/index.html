Septic pumping for assisted living facilities and group homes

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

Assisted living facilities and group homes cannot treat a septic backup as a minor inconvenience: residents, staff, and daily care routines are all on the same system. This page is for administrators, maintenance leads, and owners who need a reliable pumping plan and a clear after-hours call. It is not a licensing or survey-prep manual, and it is not the residential large-family guide. Commercial or facility-scale tanks are quoted for that system and its use. Call (239) 451-3261 with the property address and a staff contact who can meet the truck.

Why do these buildings need a planned schedule?

Occupancy is often steady, laundry and kitchens run on a shift, and many bathrooms are in use throughout the day. The U.S. EPA's general 3 to 5 year range for typical household tanks is not a good sole guide here. A recurring pump-out, timed around how full the building stays, is more useful than waiting for an odor in a resident hallway. We pump both compartments when we pump a septic tank, and we clean an effluent filter if the tank has one. That visit is still pumping and a look at what we saw, not a substitute for your own maintenance log or a regulatory inspection you may owe to another agency.

How is this priced?

The published residential starting price of $399.99 applies to a standard 1–3 bedroom home. It does not automatically apply to a facility tank. After-hours pumping is starting at $599.99 if a backup happens outside routine hours. Buried-lid locating and digging is quoted on the call. A 6-inch riser is $299.99 each if access has been a recurring problem. Drain field replacement and repairs are quoted only after we inspect the system. We will ask about tank size if you know it, last service dates, and how many buildings share the system.

What should a maintenance lead have ready?

Gate codes, a map of lids, a person who can stay with the truck, and any rules about working around meals, med passes, or sleeping hours. Tell us about tight drive aisles, oxygen deliveries, and parking that blocks lids. If records are missing, an inspection with pump-out is $549.99 when you want a current look at a tank that has not been opened in a long time, understanding that a lender or a surveyor may still have their own requirements. Routine visits can be requested through ServiceM8; we confirm the time before it is set.

What belongs in a short staff handout?

No wipes (including those labeled flushable), no grease down kitchen drains, no medications or paint in the toilet, and an immediate call to maintenance if drains slow or an odor appears. Incontinence products and paper towels are a common stress on busy bathrooms; they belong in the trash. You do not need a long training. You need fewer emergencies. If a backup has started, staff should stop water use in that area, keep residents and visitors away from spilled sewage, and call (239) 451-3261 rather than submitting an online request mid-overflow. More on that moment is at emergency septic backup.

How is this different from other care or commercial settings?

A group home may look like a large house and still be used like a small facility. An assisted living building may also have a kitchen that needs grease service, which is a different job from pumping the septic tank; see the grease trap page if that applies. Churches, schools, and RV parks have their own page, because their peak-use pattern is different. What these care settings share is the need for a named contact, a schedule, and a night-time plan. We serve these properties in Lee, Collier, and Charlotte County, plus LaBelle.

Assisted living and group homes questions

Do you pump septic tanks at assisted living facilities and group homes?

Yes, when we can reach the lids safely. Commercial or larger facility tanks are quoted for that system and its use. Call (239) 451-3261 with access notes and a staff contact.

Is the $399.99 residential starting price for a group home?

That published starting price is for a standard 1–3 bedroom home. A licensed facility or a larger shared system is quoted rather than assumed to be a residential pump-out.

What should staff do if a backup starts at night?

Stop running water in the affected area, keep residents away from spilled sewage, and call (239) 451-3261. After-hours pumping is starting at $599.99. Do not use the online booking form during an active overflow.

Can you set a recurring schedule around meals and shift changes?

Yes. Tell us when the building is least disruptive to service, and we can talk about a repeating visit.
