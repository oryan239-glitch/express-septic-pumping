Septic and grease service for food trucks and commissary kitchens

Food trucks and the commissary kitchens they share put a restaurant-style load on grease equipment and, often, on a septic tank that was never a single-family system. This page is for operators, commissary managers, and property owners who need those tanks and traps pumped on a schedule that fits service hours. It is not the general restaurant grease guide; the rules, the $695 price for a 1,000-gallon unit, and the Lee County timing details live on grease trap and interceptor pumping. Call (239) 451-3261 with the address, the equipment, and when the kitchen is quiet.

How is a commissary or food-truck kitchen different from a house tank?

A commissary may host several trucks that prep, wash, and dispose of wastewater and grease in the same building on staggered shifts. The load clusters around service windows, then drops. A house tank is not a good model for that pattern, and the published residential starting price of $399.99 for a standard 1–3 bedroom home does not automatically apply to a commissary tank. Commercial septic pumping is quoted for the tank and how it is used. If the site also has an in-ground interceptor or indoor traps, those are a separate kind of pump-out from the septic tank, and they should not be treated as the same job by habit.

What should I know about grease equipment at a commissary?

A 1,000-gallon grease trap or interceptor is $695 to pump; other sizes are quoted. We can set a recurring schedule. In Lee County, Ordinance 14-24 requires grease interceptors to be fully pumped at least every 90 calendar days. Indoor grease traps must be cleaned at least weekly or whenever grease and solids exceed 20% of the unit volume. The 20% rule applies to indoor traps, not to interceptors. Florida Statute 403.0741 addresses grease-waste service manifests. We describe those rules so operators know the calendar; this page does not claim that Express provides manifests, is a licensed grease hauler, or handles compliance reporting. For the fuller grease explanation, use the main grease page.

How should a manager plan access and timing?

Tell us about dock doors, shared parking, night cleaning crews, and whether trucks will be in the way. A commissary that is busiest at lunch may be easiest to service early in the morning or after close. If lids are buried in a paved lot or under a grease pad, locating and digging a buried lid is $100, plus $50 for each additional hour. A riser at $299.99 each can make a regular route faster. Routine, non-emergency visits can be requested through ServiceM8; we confirm the time before it is set.

What if the septic tank, not the grease unit, is the problem?

Slow drains, odors, or a backup in restrooms or floor drains can be the septic tank, a clogged outlet filter, or a field that is not taking water. Do not assume a grease pump-out will fix a septic backup, and do not assume the reverse. If sewage is overflowing, stop using water and call; after-hours pumping is $599.99, and the online form is not the right tool mid-overflow. Drain field or repair work, if it comes up, is quoted after we inspect the system.

What should truck operators be told about what goes down the drain?

Grease, wipes, and solids that belong in the trash still do not belong in a septic tank or a poorly maintained trap. A shared commissary needs a short house rule as much as a restaurant does, because one truck's shortcut becomes everyone's backup. If you also operate a sit-down dining room on the same property, keep the grease plan and the septic plan both on the calendar. We serve Lee, Collier, and Charlotte County, plus LaBelle, from our Estero shop.

Food trucks and commissary kitchens questions

Do you pump grease traps and interceptors used by food trucks?

Yes. We pump grease traps and interceptors for food service in our area, including at commissary kitchens that host food trucks. $695 covers a 1,000-gallon grease trap or interceptor; other sizes are quoted. Call (239) 451-3261.

Does the residential $399.99 starting price apply to a commissary?

No. That published starting price is for a standard 1–3 bedroom home. Commercial septic tanks and busier commissary systems are quoted for that tank and its use.

Do you provide grease-waste manifests or act as a licensed grease hauler?

This page does not claim that service. For Lee County interceptor and indoor-trap timing rules, and for what Florida law says about manifests, see grease trap and interceptor pumping.

Can we set a recurring pump-out around a commissary's closed day?

Yes. Tell us when the kitchen is quietest and how the trucks use the building, and we can talk about a schedule. After-hours pumping is $599.99 if a backup happens outside routine hours.
