What counts as a backup that should be called in immediately?

Wastewater rising in a shower, toilet, or floor drain; sewage on the floor; or a sudden, strong sewage odor with fixtures that will not drain. Gurgling alone is a warning, but standing sewage is an emergency visit, not a 'maybe next week' pump-out.

What should I do in the house while I wait?

Turn off anything that adds water: laundry, dishwashers, ice makers, and showers. Avoid flushing. Keep children and pets away from spilled sewage. If you can, note whether one fixture is affected or several, and whether the problem started after heavy rain. That information helps when you call, but it should not delay the call.

What will we do when we arrive?

We access the tank and pump it, including both the inlet and outlet compartments, and we clean the effluent filter at no charge if the tank has one. A pump-out often relieves a backup caused by a full tank. If the drain field is not taking water, we will tell you what we see; further work is quoted after inspection, not invented on the spot.

How is after-hours pricing different from a planned visit?

After-hours pumping is $599.99. Planned residential pumping starts at $399.99 (standard 1–3 bed). If you have a slow drain but no backup, you can still call, or you can request a routine time on ServiceM8 once it is clearly not an active overflow. For wet yards without an indoor overflow, see drain field not draining. If the overflow started with a storm, see backup after heavy rain. If a warning light or beeper is going, see septic alarm going off.

Emergency septic backup questions

What does after-hours septic pumping cost?

After-hours pumping is $599.99. Call (239) 451-3261. Do not use the online booking form while a backup is happening.

What should I do until someone arrives?

Stop running water, including laundry and showers. Do not keep flushing 'to see if it cleared.' If sewage is on the floor, keep people and pets away from it.

Are you open at night and on weekends?

Yes. Express is open 24 hours, 7 days a week for this kind of call.

Is after-hours service the same as a routine pump-out?

The work is still pumping the tank. The after-hours price is $599.99. Routine residential pumping starts at $399.99 (standard 1–3 bed) when it is not an after-hours emergency visit.
