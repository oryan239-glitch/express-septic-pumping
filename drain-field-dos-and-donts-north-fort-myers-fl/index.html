Drain field do's and don'ts in North Fort Myers, FL

North Fort Myers is not the City of Fort Myers, and a drain field on a neighborhood lot or on acreage north of the Caloosahatchee still has to accept whatever the house sends it, including in yards that already stay wet after a summer storm. Business 41 and Hancock Bridge Parkway are how many people get here; they are not a reason to skip the habits on this page. After a heavy rain, a low yard north of the river can stay soft for days; that is a reason to keep extra water and extra weight off the laterals, not a reason to invent a special North Fort Myers method. Keep grease out of a yard that may already be wet

Cooking oil and fats belong in the trash. In a yard that stays soft after rain, a field has less unused soil under it, so leftover grease moving out of the tank is a poor addition. We do not invent a local failure count. We treat kitchen grease as a bad fit on both compact neighborhood lots and larger rural parcels.

What should not be flushed north of the river?

Wet wipes, including flushable labels, go in the trash on every kind of North Fort Myers lot. Use biodegradable, septic-safe toilet paper. The lot size does not change that rule.

Cleaners and bacteria when the soil is already damp

Heavy doses of bleach or a harsh cleaner are a worse fit, not a better one, when a North Fort Myers yard is already wet, because those products can interrupt the organisms in the tank and in the soil. See why helpful bacteria belong in the tank and the field.

Parking, trees, and runoff on neighborhood lots versus acreage

A compact lot can hide the field along a side yard. An acreage parcel can hide it far from the house, which makes it easier to drive across without noticing. In both cases, do not park or drive on the laterals, and do not place a pool, a spa, or a structure there. Keep trees and deep roots off the field; grass is the cover. Divert gutters and irrigation away from it.

Laundry, disposals, and a regular pump-out

Space washing through the week, especially after a wet spell north of the river. A disposal adds solids; stay current on pumping. Residential pumping starts at $399.99 for a standard 1 to 3 bedroom tank, confirmed when you call. If the field needs repair, we quote after inspection.

Kitchens north of the river, and the 14-24 90-day rule where it applies

Food-service kitchens billed by Lee County Utilities follow Lee County Ordinance 14-24. Interceptors on that system have a 90-calendar-day full pump-out minimum, and indoor traps are cleaned at least weekly or whenever grease and solids go past 20% of the unit volume. The 20% rule is not for interceptors. Do not use a City of Fort Myers pretreatment page by habit for a North Fort Myers address. North Fort Myers drain field do's and don'ts questions

Is this the same as the Fort Myers drain field habit page?

No. North Fort Myers is unincorporated, north of the Caloosahatchee, and is not the City of Fort Myers. Use this page for an address here. Call (239) 451-3261.

Do neighborhood lots and acreage lots follow different flushing rules?

No. Wipes, even flushable labels, belong in the trash on both kinds of lot. Use biodegradable, septic-safe toilet paper. Access and how wet the yard gets can differ; the don'ts do not.

Should I water the drain field in the dry season to 'keep it healthy'?

No. Divert irrigation away from the laterals. The field already receives effluent. Extra spray, especially when the yard is already wet in summer, is a poor idea.
