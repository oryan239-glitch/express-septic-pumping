Food service is spread along the Tamiami Trail and in shopping and plaza clusters around Midway, Harbor, and Murdock, rather than in one historic downtown like Punta Gorda. What does Charlotte County Utilities require?

For kitchens that discharge to Charlotte County Utilities, two official documents set the pumping intervals. The Charlotte County commercial FOG practices flyer states that accumulated grease and other food waste in a grease trap must be pumped at least every 30 days, and that every interceptor shall be cleaned every 90 days or sooner, if needed. An exemption may be granted to the 90-day minimum if the user can establish that the schedule is not necessary. The Charlotte County FOG best-management guide likewise tells monitored establishments to clean and maintain a grease trap every 30 days and a grease interceptor every 90 days, to keep records of dates, action taken, volume hauled, and inspections, and to retain maintenance and hauling records no less than three years. It also tells establishments to use a licensed or permitted hauler to dispose of grease and solids. That is the facility's obligation. This page does not claim that Express is that licensed hauler, provides manifests, or keeps the three-year file.

The official Charlotte County Utilities FOG Management Program page also describes a $10 monthly FOG fee, in effect as of October 1, 2026, for establishments that use grease-management equipment. That fee is a county utility charge on the bill, not a pumping price from us. Questions can go to CCUPretreatment@charlottecountyfl.gov or 941-764-4300.

What if the Port Charlotte kitchen is still on a septic tank?

Many Port Charlotte homes, and some commercial sites, still use septic systems. The county's 30- and 90-day sewer FOG intervals apply to Charlotte County Utilities wastewater customers, not automatically to a kitchen whose interceptor discharges to a septic tank. That interceptor still needs to be pumped so grease does not overload the tank and drain field. Confirm the bill. Household septic work is on septic tank pumping in Port Charlotte.

What Florida law still applies?

Florida Statute 403.0741 requires a signed grease waste service manifest and prohibits gray-water return into the interceptor or trap. We can set a repeating route that lines up with the 30-day or 90-day county interval when it applies. After-hours pumping during an active backup is $599.99; call rather than using the form. Locating and digging a buried plaza lid is $100, plus $50 for each additional hour. See also the service-areas hub.
