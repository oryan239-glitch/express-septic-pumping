Drain field do's and don'ts in Alva, FL

In Alva, a drain field is often the normal case on a rural lot along the Caloosahatchee or State Road 80, where a tank and a well commonly share the same ground and there is no town sewer to fall back on. This page is for those houses, ranches, and farms, including older tanks that may not match a newer diagram. It is not a Buckingham page and not a Lehigh Acres grid story. Call (239) 451-3261 or request an Alva visit on ServiceM8.

Why are grease and oil a poor fit on a river-road well-and-field lot?

Oil from a river-road kitchen should be cooled and put in the can, not rinsed toward a tank that sits on the same ground as the well. Grease that leaves the tank still has to be accepted by laterals in a rural yard. This page does not offer a tally of failed Alva fields, and it does not speak for any well as if we had tested it. Keeping fats out of the drain is simply how you avoid loading that shared lot.

What should an Alva bathroom never send to the tank?

Anything sold as a wipe, flushable or not, should leave the house in a bag, not in the pipe. In an Alva bath, choose a roll made to break down in a septic tank. A long lane and a bigger household do not create a different flushing rule.

Do rural lots get a pass on harsh cleaners?

No. A leftover bottle of disinfectant is not a job for the septic line on a ranch or river lot. Those products can knock back the living treatment that happens in the tank and in the soil. Wiping down a rural bath the ordinary way is not what this warning is about. Open land is not a substitute for that biology. See the bacteria page for rural Southwest Florida tanks.

Equipment, barns, and plantings that should stay off the laterals

On a ranch or river parcel it is easy to leave a truck, a trailer, or a piece of equipment sitting on ground that happens to hold the laterals. That traffic belongs somewhere else, and so does a pool, a spa, or a barn lean-to. Keep deep-rooted plantings off that rectangle and let a lawn be the cover. Route downspouts and sprinklers onto other ground so a June downpour is not ponding on the field.

Laundry on a larger household, and a regular pump-out along SR 80

When extra family is staying along SR 80, run the machines on different days instead of one long session. A disposal contributes more solids, which is a reason not to let the tank go past due. Rural kitchens, and the 14-24 clocks only where LCU applies

Food-service sites along this stretch often sit on a tank with an outdoor interceptor and no municipal FOG desk at all. The interceptor still has to be emptied so grease does not ride into the tank. When the kitchen is actually an LCU account, it follows Lee County Ordinance 14-24. That ordinance sets a full interceptor pump-out at least once every 90 calendar days, and it treats indoor traps separately: a weekly cleaning, or sooner if grease and solids pass 20% of the trap volume. Do not read the 20% trigger as an interceptor rule. Restaurant pages are on the grease trap pumping service-areas hub and grease trap pumping in Alva. Alva drain field do's and don'ts questions

Are Alva houses typically on a drain field?

Yes, many rural properties along the Caloosahatchee and State Road 80 use a tank and a field, often with a well on the same lot. A few sites may be on Lee County Utilities; check the bill. Call (239) 451-3261.

Does a larger ranch lot make wipes or grease safer?

No. Lot size does not change what the tank can handle. Bag fats and wipes, and use a biodegradable, septic-safe roll.

Does Ordinance 14-24 cover every Alva kitchen?

Only if the kitchen discharges to Lee County Utilities. Many Alva properties use a septic tank with an outdoor interceptor and have no sewer FOG program. Confirm the bill.
