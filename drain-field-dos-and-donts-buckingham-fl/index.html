Drain field do's and don'ts in Buckingham, FL

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

Buckingham is a large-lot community east of Fort Myers, and a drain field here is usually the way the property treats its own wastewater, on acreage that can hide laterals far from the house and often includes a well on the same ground. This page is for those lots. It is not the City of Fort Myers habit page, and it is not an Alva river-road page. Call (239) 451-3261 or request a Buckingham visit on ServiceM8.

Why do kitchen fats still matter on a large lot east of Fort Myers?

A wide yard does not make oil and grease disappear. They belong in the trash, not in the drain. They collect and can move toward the field on a system that is still the only treatment on the lot. We do not invent a Buckingham failure number. We do treat kitchen grease as off-limits on these large-lot tanks.

What should not be flushed in a Buckingham bath?

Flushable marketing does not belong in a Buckingham toilet; wipes go in the garbage. Choose paper that is meant to break down in a septic tank. A long driveway and a private lane do not change what the tank can handle.

Cleaners and the soil biology under a wide yard

Cleaning a Buckingham bath the usual way is fine. Pouring out a leftover jug of bleach or a harsh cleaner is not, because it can stall the organisms in the tank and in the soil under the field. Lot size does not cancel that. See why the soil biology is part of the field.

Vehicles, paddocks, and trees on a large-lot field

Laterals on a large lot are easy to drive across, fence across, or plant over without meaning to. Keep vehicles, trailers, and livestock traffic off that rectangle. Do not place a pool, a spa, or a structure on it. Keep deep roots off the laterals; turf is the cover that belongs there. Aim gutters and irrigation away from that soil so extra water is not sitting on the same ground the field uses.

Spacing laundry, and staying current on pumping

Run laundry on more than one day instead of stacking every load together. A disposal adds more solids to the tank, which is a reason to keep the pump-out on time. Buckingham kitchens, and 14-24 only on an LCU account

A food-service kitchen here is more often tied to a tank and an outdoor interceptor than to a city sewer desk. That arrangement has no municipal FOG calendar to copy; the interceptor still has to be emptied so grease does not reach the field. If the kitchen discharges to Lee County Utilities, Lee County Ordinance 14-24 applies. Under that ordinance an interceptor must be fully emptied at least once every 90 calendar days, while an indoor trap is cleaned at least weekly, or sooner if grease and solids go past 20% of the trap volume. Do not attach the 20% trigger to interceptors. Kitchen schedules are listed from the grease trap pumping service-areas hub and grease trap pumping in Buckingham. Buckingham drain field do's and don'ts questions

Is Buckingham usually on septic?

Yes. Buckingham is unincorporated Lee County east of Fort Myers, with many large-lot tanks. A few sites may be on Lee County Utilities; check the bill. Call (239) 451-3261.

Can I plant a row of trees to hide the field?

No. Deep roots belong off the laterals. Turf is the cover that belongs there. Keep vehicles and new buildings off that footprint as well.

Does Ordinance 14-24 apply to every Buckingham kitchen?

Only when the kitchen discharges to Lee County Utilities. A kitchen on a septic tank with an outdoor interceptor has no sewer FOG calendar; still pump the interceptor to protect the tank.
