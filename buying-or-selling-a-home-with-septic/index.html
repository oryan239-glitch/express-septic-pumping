Buying or selling a home with a septic tank

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

If you are buying or selling a Southwest Florida home that uses a septic tank, a current pump-out and inspection is often the missing piece in the due-diligence file. This page is for the buyer and the seller, not for agents (agents can start with the Realtor septic guide). The goal is simple: know when the tank was last pumped, see its condition before closing, and avoid inheriting a surprise.

Why schedule a pump-out before or during the inspection period?

A tank that has not been opened in years can hide a full sludge layer, a dirty outlet filter, or a lid no one can find. Waiting until after closing leaves the new owner to discover that on a holiday weekend. A septic inspection with pump-out is $549.99 and gives both sides a current look. Residential pumping alone starts at $399.99 (standard 1–3 bed) if you only need the tank emptied and already know the system.

What should a buyer request from the seller?

Ask for pumping receipts, any county or state permits, and notes about risers or prior repairs. If those papers do not exist, treat the system as unknown and schedule the inspection visit. Buyers who have never owned a septic system can read the first-time owner guide in parallel so terms like outlet filter and drain field are familiar before walkthrough.

What should a seller do before listing?

Have the tank pumped if it is due, or if you cannot prove the last service date. If lids are buried, budget for locating and digging at $100, plus $50 for each additional hour, or install a riser ($299.99 each) so future access is easier to show. A tidy, recently serviced tank is easier to explain than a yard with no idea where the lid is.

What happens on the appointment?

We find the lid, pump the tank, and tell you what we saw. If the tank has an effluent filter, we clean it at no charge on that pump-out. We do not invent a clean bill of health for a drain field we have not been asked to evaluate in depth; drain field replacement, if it ever comes up, is quoted only after we inspect the system.

How do I book around a closing date?

Call (239) 451-3261 with your inspection deadline, or request routine service on ServiceM8. We confirm the time before it is set. If the house is already backing up, skip the booking form and call; after-hours pumping is $599.99.

Buying or selling with septic questions

Should I get a septic inspection before closing?

If the home has a septic tank and you do not have recent records, yes, an inspection with pump-out is a sound step. That service is $549.99. Call (239) 451-3261.

Is a pump-out the same as an inspection?

A pump-out empties the tank. An inspection with pump-out is a visit that includes pumping and a look at the system so you hear what we saw. Buyers and sellers often want that combined visit.

Who usually pays for the pump-out in a sale?

That is a contract question, not a rule we set. Either party can schedule and pay. We just need access to the lids and a contact who can confirm the appointment.

What if nobody can find the lid before closing?

We can locate and dig a buried lid for $100, plus $50 for each additional hour. Tell us before the visit if the lid has not been opened in years.
