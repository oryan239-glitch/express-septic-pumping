Alva is a rural community in eastern Lee County along the Caloosahatchee, and many food-service kitchens here use a septic tank with an outdoor grease interceptor rather than a municipal sewer FOG program. This page is for food-service establishments in Alva, including along State Road 80 (Palm Beach Boulevard) and at isolated commercial or farm-market sites off the river road. $695 pumps a 1,000-gallon grease trap or interceptor; other sizes are quoted. Where are Alva kitchens?

This is not a visitor strip. Food service is scattered along the highway and at sites that serve the river community, ranches, and people passing between Fort Myers and LaBelle. Lots can be large, drives can be long, and a lid may sit behind a building rather than in a marked plaza stall. We do not invent restaurant or farm-stand names as customers. Lehigh Acres kitchens to the south should use grease trap pumping in Lehigh Acres when that is the right address.

Sewer FOG rules apply only if the kitchen is on LCU sewer

Alva does not have a city-owned sewer utility writing its own grease ordinance. If a kitchen actually discharges to Lee County Utilities, Lee County Ordinance 14-24 requires interceptors to be fully pumped at least every 90 calendar days, and indoor traps to be cleaned at least weekly or whenever grease and solids exceed 20% of the unit volume. Many Alva homes, ranches, and businesses rely on septic systems. For those kitchens, there is no sewer FOG pumping calendar to copy. The outdoor interceptor still needs to be pumped so grease does not overload the septic tank and drain field. Confirm which system serves the property before you adopt a 90-day sewer interval.

What Florida law still applies on a rural lot?

Florida Statute 403.0741 still requires a signed grease waste service manifest and still prohibits returning removed grease waste to the unit, whether the discharge goes to a sewer or to a septic tank. This Alva page does not claim that Express provides manifests, is a licensed grease hauler, or handles compliance reporting. We can set a repeating route that matches 14-24 when it applies, or that matches the kitchen's actual load when the site is on septic. After-hours pumping during an active backup is $699.99–$799.99 by home size; call rather than using the form. Locating and digging a buried lid on a large lot is quoted on the call. Household septic work is on septic tank pumping in Alva. See also the service-areas hub.

River road access is part of the job, not a different price

State Road 80 follows the Caloosahatchee, and some commercial sites sit close to the river or at the end of a long drive off the highway. After a heavy rain, a low lot can be softer and a lid can be harder to see. That is useful to mention when you book; it does not change the $695 price for a 1,000-gallon unit. Alva also sits on the way to LaBelle and inland Hendry County, but this page is only for Alva addresses. A river-adjacent interceptor on a septic tank is still grease equipment, not a household septic pump-out, and not a Lee County Utilities FOG appointment unless the bill says the kitchen is on that sewer. Tell us about gates, animals, and whether the tank and the interceptor are on the same property so we bring the right plan, not a copied city-sewer script.

Sources: Lee County Ordinance 14-24 (only if the kitchen discharges to Lee County Utilities); Florida Statute 403.0741.

Alva grease trap pumping questions

Are Alva restaurants on Lee County Ordinance 14-24?

Alva is unincorporated eastern Lee County and is largely rural. If a kitchen discharges to Lee County Utilities, Ordinance 14-24 applies (interceptors at least every 90 calendar days; indoor traps weekly or when grease and solids exceed 20% of unit volume). Many Alva properties use septic tanks with outdoor grease interceptors and have no sewer FOG program. Confirm the bill. Call (239) 451-3261.
