Where are North Fort Myers kitchens?

Business 41 (the older Tamiami corridor) and Hancock Bridge Parkway hold much of the area's food service, with more kitchens along Pine Island Road toward Cape Coral. The riverfront and the bridges into downtown Fort Myers bring commuter and visitor traffic that still spikes in winter, even if this side of the river is less of a tourist strip than Fort Myers Beach. We do not name private restaurants as customers. City-of-Fort-Myers addresses should use grease trap pumping in Fort Myers; Cape Coral addresses should use Cape Coral.

What local FOG rule applies?

For kitchens that discharge to Lee County Utilities, Lee County Ordinance 14-24 is the local rule this page cites: grease interceptors must be fully pumped at least every 90 calendar days, and indoor grease traps must be cleaned at least weekly or whenever grease and solids exceed 20% of the unit volume. Do not attach the 20% rule to interceptors. Do not assume City of Fort Myers' unpublished-to-us frequency, Cape Coral Utilities' unpublished-to-us frequency, or Bonita Springs Utilities' 25% wetted-height interceptor rule unless that utility bills the account.

What Florida law still applies?

North of the Caloosahatchee, Florida Statute 403.0741 still requires a signed grease waste service manifest and still prohibits putting removed grease waste back into the interceptor or trap. Repeating routes can keep a North Fort Myers kitchen on the 90-day interceptor interval, or on a tighter indoor-trap interval, without waiting for a backup. This community page does not offer Express as a manifest writer, licensed grease hauler, or compliance desk.

North of the river is not the same jurisdiction as downtown

It is easy to treat 'Fort Myers' as one restaurant market because Hancock Bridge Parkway and the other river crossings feed downtown so quickly. For FOG rules, the line matters. North Fort Myers is unincorporated. A kitchen on Business 41 or Pine Island Road that discharges to Lee County Utilities follows Ordinance 14-24, including the 90-calendar-day interceptor interval and the weekly-or-20% indoor-trap rule. A kitchen that has already crossed into the City of Fort Myers, or west into Cape Coral, is usually under a different utility program, and those city pages are written that way on purpose. Winter traffic still builds on the bridges and on Business 41; a mid-morning weekday visit is often easier than a Friday evening. If you are not sure which side of the city limit you are on, read the utility bill or tell us the address when you call and we will help you pick the right local page, not invent a blended rule.

Sources: Lee County Ordinance 14-24; Florida Statute 403.0741.

Does Ordinance 14-24 apply in North Fort Myers?

North Fort Myers is unincorporated Lee County.
