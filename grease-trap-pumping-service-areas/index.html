How local rules differ, in brief

Lee County Ordinance 14-24 applies to food-service establishments discharging to Lee County Utilities: interceptors fully pumped at least every 90 calendar days; indoor traps cleaned at least weekly or whenever grease and solids exceed 20% of unit volume. The Bonita Springs Utilities Sewer Use Policy (BOD approved August 2019) requires interceptors completely evacuated at least every 90 days, or sooner if 25% or more of the wetted height contains floating materials, sediment, or FOG. The Collier County Food Service Establishments Program requires indoor traps at least every 30 days and interceptors at least every 90 days for kitchens in the county program on the county wastewater system. City of Fort Myers, City of Cape Coral, City of Naples, City of Marco Island, City of Sanibel, City of Punta Gorda, Ave Maria Utility Company, and Immokalee Water & Sewer District administer their own programs; where we could not independently verify a published pumping frequency, the city pages say to confirm the schedule with that utility rather than inventing a number. Charlotte County Utilities' official commercial FOG flyer and best-management guide require grease traps to be pumped at least every 30 days and interceptors cleaned every 90 days or sooner, with a possible exemption from the 90-day minimum, for county sewer customers. In rural communities such as Golden Gate Estates, Pine Island, Alva, and Buckingham, many kitchens use a septic tank and an outdoor grease interceptor with no sewer FOG calendar; those pages say so rather than copying a sewer ordinance onto a drain field.

Florida Statute 403.0741 applies statewide. It requires a signed grease waste service manifest for every pump-out and prohibits gray-water return. This hub does not claim that Express provides manifests, is a licensed grease hauler, or handles compliance reporting.
