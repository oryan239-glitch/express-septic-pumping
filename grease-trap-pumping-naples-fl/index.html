Where are Naples food-service kitchens concentrated?

Downtown and the historic districts draw a heavy winter and weekend crowd. U.S. 41 carries a long commercial strip of independent and center-based kitchens. Seasonal occupancy, special events, and visitors staying on the Gulf all push more plates through those kitchens than a quiet October weekday. Pump when the unit is due, and try not to let the interval land on a Saturday in high season if you can book earlier. We do not publish private restaurant names as customers here. North of the city, North Naples grease trap pumping may be the better local page.

What if the kitchen discharges to Collier County sewer?

The official Collier County Food Service Establishments Program states that indoor grease traps, usually inside the building or under a sink, must be pumped at least every 30 days, and that grease interceptors, usually below ground outside, must be pumped at least every 90 days. Establishments must keep cleaning and service records for at least three years, including service date, hauler name, disposal site, and volume or condition pumped, and make them available for county inspection. The county says it does not maintain a list of approved haulers. Those published intervals apply to food-service establishments in the county program, which is aimed at kitchens that discharge to Collier County's wastewater system.

What if the kitchen is on City of Naples sewer?

City of Naples has its own Utilities Department. The city's sewer-use regulations require grease, oil, and sand interceptors when the city determines they are necessary, require the owner to maintain records of removal and disposal, and require that hauling of collected material not performed by the owner's personnel be done by a currently licensed waste disposal firm. During this research we did not independently verify a City of Naples ordinance text that sets a specific 30-day or 90-day pumping interval for city sewer customers. Do not assume the county's 30- and 90-day intervals automatically apply to a City of Naples utility account. Confirm the current schedule with City of Naples Utilities.

What statewide rules apply in either case?

Florida Statute 403.0741 has two statewide points for a Naples kitchen: a signed grease waste service manifest belongs with each pump-out, and gray-water return into the interceptor or trap is prohibited. Chapter 62-705, Florida Administrative Code, covers handling, storage, and disposal of FOG. We mention those rules so operators know they exist; we are not offering manifest preparation, hauler licensing, or compliance reporting on this page. Once you know which local interval applies, we can put the kitchen on a repeating route.

Naples grease trap pumping questions

Do City of Naples and Collier County use the same grease pumping schedule?

Not automatically. Collier County's published program for kitchens on county sewer is at least every 30 days for indoor grease traps and at least every 90 days for interceptors. For kitchens on City of Naples sewer, confirm the current schedule with City of Naples Utilities. Call (239) 451-3261.

How much does grease trap pumping cost in Naples?

Pumping a 1,000-gallon grease trap or interceptor in Naples is $695. Other capacities are quoted.

Does Express provide grease-waste manifests?

No. Florida Statute 403.0741 governs manifests and gray-water return; this Naples page is not a claim that we supply that paperwork or act as a licensed grease hauler.
