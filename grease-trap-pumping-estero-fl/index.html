Why do Lee County Utilities and Bonita Springs Utilities both matter in Estero?

Estero does not have a single village-owned sewer utility for every kitchen. BSU franchise service area states that BSU's franchise includes a portion of the Village of Estero (as well as the City of Bonita Springs and unincorporated south Lee County). Other Estero kitchens discharge to Lee County Utilities. The pumping interval you must meet follows the utility that actually receives the wastewater, so look at the bill or call the utility if you are not sure.

What if the kitchen is on Lee County Utilities?

Lee County Ordinance 14-24 requires grease interceptors to be fully pumped at least every 90 calendar days. Indoor grease traps must be cleaned at least weekly or whenever grease and solids exceed 20% of the unit volume. The 20% rule is for indoor traps, not for interceptors. That ordinance applies to food-service establishments discharging to Lee County Utilities' wastewater system.

What if the kitchen is on Bonita Springs Utilities?

The Bonita Springs Utilities Sewer Use Policy (BOD approved August 2019) requires grease interceptors to be completely evacuated at least every 90 days, or sooner if 25% or more of the wetted height contains floating materials, sediment, or FOG. Complete removal of grease, water, and solids is required, and pump-and-return is prohibited. BSU's maintenance card also asks facilities to send manifests or receipts to BSU within 30 days. That is the kitchen's paperwork duty. This page does not claim that Express provides manifests, is a licensed grease hauler, or handles compliance reporting. More Bonita-specific detail is on grease trap pumping in Bonita Springs.

What statewide rule still applies either way?

Under Florida Statute 403.0741, each pump-out needs a signed grease waste service manifest, and removed grease waste may not be returned to the unit.
