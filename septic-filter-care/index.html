Septic filter care and drain field protection

Open 24 hours, 7 days · Owner-operated from Estero · Dispatch confirms every appointment by phone or text.

If your septic tank has an effluent filter, keeping it clean helps wastewater leave the tank as it should. This page explains how to access the filter, when to check it, and how healthy bacteria in the soil help protect the drain field.

How often should I clean my septic effluent filter?

If your tank has an effluent filter, it should be checked and cleaned at every pump-out and whenever drains slow. Express can advise on a schedule for your system. Call (239) 451-3261.

How do I access the effluent filter?

The effluent filter is in the outlet side of the tank, under the lid.

Why does a riser help?

A 6-inch riser ($299.99 each) brings the lid up to ground level, so the filter is easier to reach on later visits without digging.

What can happen with a dirty filter?

A dirty filter can cause backups or slow drains, and wastewater can be pushed back into the house. Express cleans your filter on pump-outs and can show you how to keep it clean.

How healthy soil bacteria protect the drain field

Healthy bacteria in the soil break down effluent after it leaves the tank, which helps protect the drain field. Harsh chemicals, solids and grease overload can hurt that process. Read more about why good bacteria protect your septic tank and drain field. If a drain field has already failed, see drain field replacement.
