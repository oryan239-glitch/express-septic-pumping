Septic pumping for mobile home parks

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

Mobile and manufactured home parks put a different set of access and scheduling needs on a septic visit than a single house on its own lot. Some communities have individual tanks at each home. Others have shared or community systems. This page is for park owners, managers, and residents who need pumping, lid access, or help after a backup. It is not a page for RV dump stations at a campground; those sites are closer to our churches, schools, and RV parks guide. Call (239) 451-3261 with the park name, the address, and who will meet the truck.

How is a park different from a single-family house?

Space between homes can be tight. Lids may sit under carports, pavers, or utility pedestals. A manager may be coordinating several tanks on the same day, or one shared system that serves many households. Residents may not know where the lid is, or whether the tank is theirs to schedule. Those details change how we plan the visit. They do not change the basic work: we need safe access, we pump the tank, and we tell you what we saw. If a park tank has an effluent filter, we clean it at no charge on that pump-out.

Who should call — the resident or the park?

Follow the park's rule, but make sure the person who calls can unlock gates, move vehicles, and point to lid locations or at least the lot number. A resident who smells sewage should tell the office immediately rather than waiting, and should stop running water if a backup has started. Managers who want a written log of dates and lots can treat that the same way property managers and HOAs keep a simple service record. We do not settle disputes about who pays; we need a clear customer for the appointment and a way to reach the lids.

How is pumping priced for park or shared systems?

A planned residential pump-out starts at $399.99 for a standard 1–3 bedroom home. That starting price does not automatically apply to a shared park system, a larger community tank, or a route of many lots. Those visits are quoted for the tank and the use. After-hours pumping is $599.99 if a unit or a common system backs up outside routine hours. Buried lids still use the published locating price: $100, plus $50 for each additional hour. If you want a recurring route, call and we can talk about a schedule around how full the park stays in season.

What access and scheduling issues should a manager plan for?

Parked cars over lids, locked storage sheds, pets, and low branches all slow a visit. A map of tank locations, even a simple one, is more useful than a verbal 'it is around back' when staff changes. Tell us about soft ground after rain, which matters in parks with tight streets and a high water table. If several lots are due, try to group them so the truck is not threading the same community four times in a month without a plan. Routine, non-emergency work can also be requested through ServiceM8; we still confirm the time before it is set.

What should residents be told?

A short list is enough: no wipes, no grease, no paint or medications down the drains, and call the office (or us, if that is the park's instruction) when drains slow or an odor appears. Residents who are new to septic tanks can be pointed to the first-time septic guide. If a backup is happening now, they should stop using water and someone should call (239) 451-3261, not submit an online request while sewage is in a tub. Drain field or repair work, if a community field is in trouble, is quoted after we inspect the system.

Mobile home parks questions

Do you pump septic tanks in mobile home parks?

Yes. We pump tanks that serve mobile and manufactured home communities in our service area when we can reach the lids safely. Call (239) 451-3261 with the park address, access notes, and who should meet the truck.

Is the residential $399.99 starting price for a shared park system?

No. That published starting price is for a standard 1–3 bedroom home. Shared, community, or larger commercial tanks are quoted for that system and its use.

Who should place the call, the resident or the park office?

If the tank is the park's responsibility, the manager or owner should call and arrange access. If a lot has its own tank under a resident's agreement, the resident or the park can schedule, but someone must be able to show lids and unlock gates.

A unit is backing up after hours. What then?

Stop running water and call (239) 451-3261. After-hours pumping is $599.99. Do not use the online booking form during an active backup.
