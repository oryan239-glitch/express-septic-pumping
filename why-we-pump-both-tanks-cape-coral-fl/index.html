Why we pump both septic tank compartments in Cape Coral, FL

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

Cape Coral began as a low-density community on septic tanks and shallow wells, and many North Cape houses still have those tanks, so both chambers still need to be emptied until the street is actually on city sewer. Streets that already have city utilities do not have an inlet or outlet septic chamber. This page is for the lots that still do, including canal-front parcels. We pump both compartments, and we clean the filter when one is present. Call (239) 451-3261 or request a Cape Coral pump-out on ServiceM8.

Which lots still have chambers to empty, and which do not?

The City's Utilities Extension Project has brought water and sewer to much of the Cape and continues working north. Look at the bill rather than assuming your block still has a tank, or assuming it does not. If you pay a city sewer charge, this page is not for your house. If you do not, you likely still have a tank; we will empty both the inlet and the outlet when the tank is built that way. Connection and abandonment questions go to City Customer Billing Services at (239) 242-3853. We do not invent a completion date for your street.

Canal lots, shallow wells, and what a skipped outlet can cause

The City has noted that failed septic effluent can reach groundwater and canals. The inlet chamber is where solids should be held. The outlet chamber is the last place those solids should be allowed to remain. A pump-out that stops at the inlet can leave sludge and scum in the outlet, where they can move toward a drain field that shares the lot with a well or sits beside a canal, or pack a filter and send wastewater back into the house. Cleaning a filter later does not retrieve solids that already left the tank. Emptying both chambers is the step we treat as standard, not optional, while the lot still depends on that system.

Access on canal-side pads

Two compartments usually mean two lids, and on a canal pad those covers can sit in a tight side yard, under a dock-side enclosure, or under growth along the seawall. Tell us about access, including a boat lift or a narrow easement, when you book. If a lid has to be located and uncovered, that work is $100, plus $50 for each extra hour. Reaching the second lid is how the outlet gets emptied, not a different service line.

What to ask while you wait on sewer, and what the visit includes

Ask whether both chambers will be pumped, and whether the filter will be cleaned if the tank has one. We include that filter cleaning in the pump-out and can demonstrate how it seats. We start residential pumping at $399.99 for a standard 1 to 3 bedroom tank and confirm the amount for yours by phone. After-hours pumping during an active backup is $599.99; call. Cape Coral both-compartment pumping questions

Which Cape Coral streets still have two septic chambers to pump?

Many streets already have city sewer, especially south of Pine Island Road as the Utilities Extension Project has moved through the city. Many North Cape homes still have a tank. If you do not pay a city sewer charge, you likely have a tank; we empty both compartments when it is built that way. Call (239) 451-3261. For connection questions, the City points customers to Customer Billing Services at (239) 242-3853.

Until my street is on city sewer, do both compartments still need to be pumped?

Yes. The tank you have now still has to work. Waiting on the Utilities Extension Project is not a reason to leave the outlet chamber loaded.

Do you clean the filter when you pump both sides on the Cape?

Yes, when a filter is present. That cleaning is included in the pump-out, not billed as a separate filter job.
