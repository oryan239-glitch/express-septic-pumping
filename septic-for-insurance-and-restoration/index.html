Express Septic Tank Pumping

Septic pumping for insurance and restoration companies

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

When a house on a septic tank has a sewage backup, the restoration crew and the adjuster often need the tank pumped before the indoor work can move, or they need to know whether the tank was simply full. This page is for those coordination calls. We pump the tank and tell you what we saw. We are not a restoration contractor, and this page does not claim that we file insurance claims or automatically bill a carrier. Call (239) 451-3261 with the address, a site contact, and whether sewage is still coming up.

What should happen first on a backup that may become a claim?

The occupants should stop running water and stay away from spilled sewage. Someone should call us if the tank has not been opened yet. After-hours pumping is $599.99. Do not use the online booking form during an active overflow. The homeowner-facing steps are also on emergency septic backup. If the overflow started with a storm, backup after heavy rain describes that pattern. Pumping the tank is often the first mechanical step; it is not the indoor drying scope.

What will Express do, and what will we not do?

We locate the lid if needed, pump both compartments, and clean the effluent filter at no charge if the tank has one. Locating and digging a buried lid is $100, plus $50 for each additional hour. We tell the site contact what we observed. We do not pull wet drywall, run dehumidifiers, pack out contents, or write a cause-and-origin report in the insurance sense. If the drain field appears to be part of the problem, further work is quoted after we inspect the system, not estimated from a claim note. A planned residential pump-out, when the visit is not an after-hours emergency overflow, starts at $399.99 for a standard 1–3 bedroom home.

How should payment and authorization be handled?

Be prepared to tell us who the customer is before we roll. Do not assume we will bill the insurance company directly. The owner, the restoration company, or another authorized party should arrange payment according to the published prices that apply to the visit. If you need a simple description of those prices for a file, the Southwest Florida septic service cost guide lists what we publish and what we only quote after inspection. We can discuss the visit with an adjuster on site; we still need a clear authorization.

What information helps us and helps your file?

The last known pump-out date, whether an alarm sounded, whether the backup followed heavy rain, and whether one fixture or the whole house was involved. Photos of the yard are useful for your own file, but we will not quote a new drain field from a photograph. If the house is in a claim because of a storm or a flood, wait until lids are reachable and the lot is not under standing water; see hurricane and flood septic prep.

How do we schedule once the overflow has stopped?

If the backup is under control and you need a planned follow-up pump-out or an inspection with pump-out ($549.99), call or request a routine time on ServiceM8. We confirm the appointment. We serve these coordination calls in Lee, Collier, and Charlotte County, plus LaBelle, 24 hours a day.

Insurance and restoration questions

Can an adjuster or a restoration company call you to pump a tank after a sewage backup?

Yes. Call (239) 451-3261 with the property address, a site contact, and whether sewage is still overflowing. After-hours pumping is $599.99.

Do you bill the insurance company directly?

Do not assume that we do. The property owner or the restoration company should be prepared to arrange payment unless a different arrangement is agreed before we roll. We will be clear about the published prices that apply.

What work do you perform on a backup call?

We access and pump the septic tank, including both compartments, and we clean an effluent filter at no charge if the tank has one. We tell you what we saw. We do not perform indoor demolition, drying, or contents restoration.

Is a pump-out the same as a drain field replacement?

No. A pump-out may relieve a backup caused by a full tank. Replacement or repair of a drain field is quoted only after we inspect the system.
