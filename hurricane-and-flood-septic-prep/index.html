Hurricane and flood septic prep

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

A hurricane or a long flood changes how you should treat a septic system, but it does not require a special ritual for every storm on the map. This page is about when to pump beforehand, what not to do while the yard is underwater, and how to come back to the system safely. It is not a promise of emergency response times, and it is not a rule that every house must be pumped the day a watch is issued.

What should I do before a storm if I have time?

If the tank is due, or you know it is close to full, schedule a pump-out before heavy rain if we can reach you. A tank with room in it is less likely to overflow when the water table rises. Residential pumping starts at $399.99 (standard 1–3 bed). If you just had the tank pumped, you do not need a second trip just because a forecast changed. Call (239) 451-3261 if you want a straight answer for your last service date.

What should I do while the lot is flooded?

Do not run water into a system whose tank or drain field is under standing water. Keep vehicles off the field. If you have a pump, alarm, or electrical components at a lift station, treat water and power as a safety issue and do not wade in to inspect them yourself. More on indoor backups is at emergency septic backup.

What should I do after the water goes down?

Wait until you can reach the lids without standing in floodwater. Then have the system checked before you return to full household use, especially if the house took on water or the field stayed submerged. We pump both compartments when we pump, and we clean the effluent filter at no charge. Damage beyond a full tank, including drain field work, is quoted after we inspect, not estimated from a storm report.

Why does flooding matter so much here?

Southwest Florida yards often sit on sandy soil with a water table that is already high in the rainy season. A storm surge or long rain on top of that can saturate a drain field that was working in dry weather. Pumping when you are due is one of the few steps you can take in advance. Afterward, patience and an inspection matter more than rushing water through a still-soggy field.

Hurricane and flood prep questions

Should I always pump the tank before every named storm?

Not automatically. If a pump-out is already due, or the tank is close to full, pumping before a storm can help. If the tank was recently emptied, a special trip is not a rule we invent for every forecast. Call (239) 451-3261 if you are unsure.

Can I use the septic system while the yard is flooded?

Do not use it while standing water covers the tank or drain field. Wait until the water recedes, then have the system checked before you return to normal use.

What should I do after a hurricane or flood?

Stay off a saturated drain field, watch for electrical hazards if you have pumps or an alarm, and call us when you can reach the lids safely. We will tell you what we see; we do not quote a repair from a storm photo alone.

We are open 24 hours, 7 days a week. After-hours pumping is $599.99. Call (239) 451-3261.
