In Cape Coral, many lots south of Pine Island Road are already on city sewer, but a house that is still on a tank — including many in North Cape while the Utilities Extension Project proceeds — still has a drain field that can be harmed by grease, wipes, or a trailer parked on the laterals. A UEP phase on a map does not retire those habits early. Confirm the bill, then treat this page as a household checklist, not a sewer-connection guide. Questions about connecting go to the City. Until the street is on city sewer, the kitchen still feeds the field

Keep oil and fats out of the sink. They collect in the tank and can move toward the field, including on canal-front lots where the system already works close to the water. Cape Coral began as a community of septic tanks and shallow wells; even where wells have been replaced, a remaining field still has to accept what leaves the house. We do not invent a count of canal fields damaged by grease. We do treat it as a don't.

What should not go in a Cape toilet?

Wet wipes, including flushable labels, belong in the trash. Use biodegradable, septic-safe toilet paper. A house waiting on a UEP connection is not a reason to relax that rule for a year or two.

Cleaners and the bacteria that treat effluent in canal-side soil

A leftover jug of bleach or a harsh cleaner does not belong in a single pour down a Cape drain. Ordinary cleaning is fine. See the septic-bacteria guide.

Trailers, pools, roots, and runoff on a canal lot

Do not park or drive on the field, and do not use that strip as boat-trailer storage. Keep pools, spas, and buildings off the laterals. Deep roots do not belong on that footprint; turf is the cover. Aim downspouts and sprinklers elsewhere so canal-side groundwater and rain are not joined by extra spray on the same soil.

Laundry, disposals, and pumping while you wait on sewer

Spread laundry through the week. A disposal adds solids; keep pumping current rather than stretching the calendar because a sewer project is 'coming.' Cape kitchens, and when Ordinance 14-24 actually applies

Restaurants on Cape Coral Utilities follow the city's FOG program; confirm the current schedule with the city rather than inventing a number here. Lee County Ordinance 14-24 applies to kitchens discharging to Lee County Utilities, not automatically to a city-sewer Cape account. On LCU, interceptors have a 90-calendar-day full pump-out minimum, and indoor traps are cleaned at least weekly or whenever grease and solids exceed 20% of the unit volume. The 20% rule is not for interceptors. Kitchen schedules are listed from the grease trap pumping service-areas hub and grease trap pumping in Cape Coral. Cape Coral drain field do's and don'ts questions

If my Cape street is on the Utilities Extension Project, do these habits still apply?

Yes, until the house is connected to city sewer and the tank is abandoned. A UEP phase on the map does not make wipes, grease, or parking on the field safe in the meantime. Call (239) 451-3261.

Do canal-front lots have a different flushing rule?

The don'ts are the same. Wipes, even flushable labels, belong in the trash. Use biodegradable, septic-safe toilet paper. A canal lot just has less room to send leftover solids or compacted soil toward the water.

Can I park a boat trailer on the drain field if the grass looks dry?

No. Vehicles compact the soil over the laterals whether the grass looks dry or not. Keep trailers and cars off that footprint.
