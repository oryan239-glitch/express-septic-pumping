Why wet-season groundwater makes ordinary mistakes more expensive

When the water table comes up, there is less unsaturated soil under many North Naples fields. That is a setting, not a new method. Keep fats and oils out of the sink. They belong in the trash, not in a disposal rinse. We do not invent a failure count for this corridor. We do treat grease as a don't because the field already has little freeboard in summer.

What should not go in a North Naples toilet?

Wet wipes, including flushable packaging, should stay in the trash. Use biodegradable, septic-safe toilet paper. A larger-lot house can hide how much has been flushed; the tank still has to handle it, and the field still receives whatever gets past the outlet.

Bleach, bacteria, and a yard that is already damp

Heavy pours of disinfectant can knock back the organisms that work in the tank and in the drain-field soil. That matters more, not less, when the yard is already wet. Ordinary cleaning is fine; dumping leftover product is not. See the page on bacteria in the tank and the soil.

Irrigation, parking, and trees on a larger North Naples lot

A wider yard can make the field harder to see, and easier to drive across or to cover with a parking pad. Do not park or drive on it. Do not place a pool, a spa, or a building on that footprint. Keep trees and deep-rooted shrubs off the laterals; grass is the cover. Point gutters and irrigation away from the field so you are not soaking the same soil that has to accept effluent.

Laundry and disposals when the household is full

Spread laundry through the week, especially when winter occupancy returns. A garbage disposal adds solids; use it lightly and keep pumping current rather than stretching the interval. North Naples drain field do's and don'ts questions

Why does a high water table change drain field habits in North Naples?

It does not change the list of don'ts, but it leaves less unused soil under the field in the rainy season, so grease, a skipped pump-out, or extra water on the laterals has less margin. Call (239) 451-3261.

Are wet wipes acceptable if I only use them now and then?

No. Keep wipes out of the toilet, including flushable labels. Use biodegradable, septic-safe toilet paper, and throw wipes away.

Can I run a sprinkler zone over the drain field to keep the grass green?

Aim irrigation away from the field. The laterals already put water into that soil. Extra spray, especially in the wet season, is a poor idea.
