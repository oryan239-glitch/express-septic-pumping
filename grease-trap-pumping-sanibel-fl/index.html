Sanibel kitchens work on a barrier island with a city sewer utility, a single causeway, and a winter season that changes both covers and truck access. This page is for food-service establishments on Sanibel, including along Periwinkle Way and in the commercial pockets that serve beach and refuge visitors. On the island, a 1,000-gallon grease trap or interceptor is $695 to pump; other sizes are quoted. Call (239) 451-3261 or book a planned Sanibel visit on ServiceM8.

What is distinctive about restaurant service on Sanibel?

Almost all visitor and service traffic uses the Sanibel Causeway. Midday in season is a poor window for a pumper truck; early morning or a midweek slot is usually kinder to the kitchen and to the causeway queue. That is an access issue, not a price change. Captiva is the next island north, across the Blind Pass Bridge; Captiva does not have a group-2 grease page in this batch, so call if the kitchen is there and we will talk about access rather than invent a separate ordinance. We do not list private restaurants as customers.

Which FOG program applies on the island?

The City of Sanibel operates the sanitary sewer system for typical city customers. The city's official City of Sanibel Fat Free Sewers page explains that grease from household drains and from poorly maintained grease traps in restaurants and other businesses can block sewers, and it tells readers not to pour cooled fryer fat down the drain. That page does not, in the text we could retrieve, set a numeric pumping interval such as every 30 days or every 90 days. During this research we did not independently verify a city ordinance section we could quote for a required frequency. Confirm the current indoor-trap and interceptor schedule with City of Sanibel Utilities. Do not assume Lee County Ordinance 14-24 automatically applies to a city-sewer account; 14-24 is for kitchens discharging to Lee County Utilities.

What Florida law still applies?

Florida Statute 403.0741 still requires a signed grease waste service manifest for the pump-out and still prohibits gray-water return, including discharging removed grease waste back into the interceptor or trap. This Sanibel page is about pumping, island access, and pointing you to the city utility; it is not a claim that Express issues manifests, holds grease-hauler licensing, or files compliance reports. After you confirm the city's current interval, we can match a repeating route to it.

Island lots, lids, and after-hours calls

After-hours pumping during an active backup is $699.99–$799.99 by home size; call, and do not rely on the online form while a line is overflowing. Sanibel grease trap pumping questions

What grease pumping schedule does the City of Sanibel require?

The city operates sanitary sewer and publishes Fat Free Sewers guidance that mentions grease traps in restaurants, but during this research we did not independently verify an official city pumping frequency such as 30 or 90 days. Confirm the current schedule with City of Sanibel Utilities. Call (239) 451-3261.

How much does grease trap pumping cost on Sanibel?

On the island, a 1,000-gallon grease trap or interceptor is $695 to pump. Other sizes are quoted.

Does Lee County Ordinance 14-24 automatically apply on Sanibel?

No. Ordinance 14-24 applies to food-service establishments discharging to Lee County Utilities. A kitchen on City of Sanibel sewer follows the city's program. Confirm the provider on the bill.

Can you schedule around Causeway traffic and tourist season?

Yes. Mention the Causeway and the kitchen's quietest hour when you call (239) 451-3261, or use ServiceM8 for a planned visit.
