Southwest Florida septic service cost guide

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

This page collects the septic and grease prices Express actually publishes, and it is honest about the work we only quote after we look at the system. It is not a market survey of other companies, and it does not invent an 'average Southwest Florida septic bill.' If a number is not on this list, we do not have a published price for it. For a planned residential visit, you can call (239) 451-3261 or request a time on ServiceM8.

What does a planned residential pump-out cost?

A planned residential pump-out starts at $399.99 for a standard 1–3 bedroom home. That is the published starting point for emptying a household tank on a routine visit. It is not automatically the price for a large commercial tank, a shared park system, a hotel, or a venue. Those visits are quoted for the tank and its use. We pump both the inlet and the outlet compartments, and we clean the effluent filter at no charge if the tank has one. If you only remember one household number, this is the one for a scheduled, non-emergency pump-out that fits that standard starting point.

What does after-hours pumping cost?

After-hours pumping is $599.99. Use this when sewage is overflowing, or when you need that after-hours visit, and call rather than booking online. The steps to take in the house are on emergency septic backup. After-hours pricing is not a different kind of pumping; it is the published price for that timing.

What does a septic inspection with pump-out cost?

A septic inspection with pump-out is $549.99. That is the visit buyers, sellers, and some lenders ask for when they want the tank emptied and a report of what we saw. It is still not a general home inspection or an FHA or VA certification; see FHA, VA, and lender septic requirements and septic inspections. Choose this when records are thin or a contract deadline is in play, not only when the tank is due.

What do risers and buried-lid locating cost?

A 6-inch riser is $299.99 each. A riser brings the lid up to ground level so later visits do not start with a shovel. Locating and digging a buried lid is $100, plus $50 for each additional hour. Those access prices are in addition to the pump-out or inspection when both are needed. The longer explanation of buried lids is at can't find the septic tank lid, and riser work is described on septic tank riser installation.

What does grease trap or interceptor pumping cost?

$695 pumps a 1,000-gallon grease trap or interceptor. Other sizes are quoted. We can set a recurring schedule. We do not extrapolate a per-gallon rate from that number. Restaurant and food-service details, including Lee County timing rules, are on grease trap and interceptor pumping. Food trucks and commissaries can start at food trucks and commissary kitchens.

What work is only quoted after an inspection?

Lift station pumping is quoted after we inspect the system; see lift station pumping. Drain field replacement and septic repairs are also quoted after we inspect; see drain field replacement and the comparison page on pumping versus drain field replacement. We will not invent a typical replacement total, a per-foot field price, or a package discount on this guide. If you need a number for that work, it comes after we look at the property. We serve Lee, Collier, and Charlotte County, plus LaBelle, from 23357 Olde Meadowbrook Circle, Estero, FL 34134.
