Cape Coral began as a canal community on septic tanks and shallow wells, and the houses that still have a tank still have an effluent filter to keep clear, especially north of Pine Island Road. Through the City's Utilities Extension Project, water and sewer have already reached nearly all of the Cape south of Pine Island Road, with work continuing north, as our Cape Coral septic pumping page describes. Until your street is connected, the tank and its outlet filter are still in service. We clean that filter when we pump, and we can show you how to keep it clear. Which streets still have a filter, and which do not?

If you already pay for city sewer, you are not looking for a septic outlet filter. If you do not, you are probably still on a tank. That is the common situation in much of the North Cape while later UEP phases proceed. We will not invent a completion date here; the City publishes those project timelines, and questions about connection or tank abandonment go to the City's Customer Billing Services at (239) 242-3853. A project that is still in construction is not a reason to ignore a slow drain this week.

Canal lots and what a dirty filter can cause

The City has noted that failed septic effluent can flow into groundwater and canals, which matters on canal-front lots. A clean filter is not a substitute for a sound drain field, and it is not a canal-protection permit. It is the screen that is supposed to keep solids in the tank so the field, and the ground around it, are not asked to take sludge. When the filter clogs, the more immediate problem is often inside the house: slow sinks, gurgling, or wastewater coming back into a shower. Clean the filter at every pump-out, and clean it again if drains slow. We do not publish a numbered Cape-wide interval.

Access on canal-side pads

Some tanks sit between the house and the seawall, with little room for a truck and a lid that may be under grass or a walkway. The filter is under the outlet lid. If we have to locate and dig, the charge is quoted on the call. A 6-inch riser at $299.99 each makes later checks possible without digging next to the canal. Tell us about seawall access, boats on lifts, and driveway width when you book. If a backup is already in progress after hours, pumping is $699.99–$799.99 by home size; call.

Until the street is on city sewer

Filter care does not change the UEP connection process. It keeps the system you have now from surprising you while you wait. General method is on septic filter care. Fort Myers and North Fort Myers have their own filter pages; see the filter service-areas hub.

Shallow wells were part of the original Cape, and the lot still has to work as a system

Cape Coral was laid out with septic tanks and shallow wells. Even as city water and sewer move north, a house that is still on a tank is still a complete on-site system until the day it is connected and the tank is abandoned. Keeping the outlet filter clear is not a well-repair service, and we do not invent a contamination figure. It is a way to keep solids in the tank so the drain field, which sits in the same canal-cut neighborhood, is not asked to take sludge while you wait on the City's project calendar. If you just purchased a North Cape house and have no pumping records, start with a pump-out so you can see the filter and the tank together.

Cape Coral filter cleaning questions

Which parts of Cape Coral still need effluent filter cleaning?

Many streets south of Pine Island Road are already on city sewer. Many North Cape homes still have septic tanks while the Utilities Extension Project works north. If you do not pay a city sewer charge, you likely have a tank and, if it was built with one, an outlet filter. Call (239) 451-3261.

Why do canal-front lots need a clear filter?

The City has noted that failed septic effluent can reach groundwater and canals. The filter's job is to keep solids in the tank. Clean it at every pump-out and whenever drains slow.

Do you clean the filter on a Cape pump-out?

Yes, and we can show you how to keep it clear.

What do risers cost in Cape Coral?
