Why good bacteria protect your septic tank and drain field

Call (239) 451-3261Book routine service

Good bacteria do two jobs in a septic system. In the tank, anaerobic bacteria break down solids. In the drain field, aerobic bacteria and a living layer called the biomat treat effluent before it reaches groundwater. Regular pumping and a clean effluent filter help protect that balance. Express cleans your filter at no charge on pump-outs.

What do anaerobic bacteria do in the septic tank?

Wastewater from the house flows into the tank. Anaerobic bacteria, which work without oxygen, break down some of the solids. Heavier material settles to the bottom as sludge. Lighter material, including grease, floats to the top as scum. The clearer wastewater in the middle, called effluent, moves on to the drain field.

How do aerobic bacteria and the biomat treat effluent in the drain field?

Effluent leaving the tank still needs treatment. In the drain-field soil, aerobic bacteria, which need oxygen, continue breaking down what remains. A thin, living layer called the biomat forms in the soil around the drain-field pipes. Together, the soil bacteria and the biomat treat effluent before it moves farther through the soil and toward groundwater.

Why does this matter in Southwest Florida?

Much of Southwest Florida has sandy soils and a high water table, especially in the rainy season. Sandy soil can let water move quickly, and a high water table leaves less unsaturated soil between the drain field and groundwater. That makes it more important to keep bacteria in the tank and the drain-field biomat healthy, and to pump the tank so solids and grease do not overload the field.

What kills or overloads septic bacteria?

Some household products and habits can kill helpful bacteria or send more solids and water to the drain field than it can treat:

  • Bleach and harsh cleaners in large amounts
  • Antibacterial soaps
  • Drain openers
  • Antibiotics and other medications flushed down the toilet or drain
  • Grease
  • Wipes, even ones labeled flushable
  • Heavy use of a garbage disposal
  • Too much water at once, such as doing all the laundry in one day

See our septic system maintenance tips for more on what not to flush or pour down drains.

What are signs the bacterial balance is off?

Odors, slow drains, soggy spots in the yard, and unusually lush grass over the drain field can mean the system is out of balance. Those signs can also have other causes. If you notice them, call (239) 451-3261.

What daily habits help?

  • Keep grease, wipes, paper towels, and medications out of the drains and toilet
  • Use household cleaners in normal amounts rather than large amounts of bleach or drain opener
  • Spread laundry loads through the week instead of doing them all in one day
  • Go easy on the garbage disposal
  • Have the tank pumped on a regular schedule, and have the effluent filter cleaned when the tank is pumped

Why does regular pumping matter for bacteria?

Bacteria in the tank cannot fully break down all of the solids. Regular septic tank pumping removes the sludge and scum that build up so those solids are less likely to leave the tank and reach the drain field, where they can clog soil and harm the biomat.

How does effluent filter cleaning help?

If your tank has an effluent filter, a clean filter helps keep solids out of the drain field so soil bacteria and the biomat can do their job. Express cleans your filter at no charge on pump-outs and can show you how to keep it clean. See effluent filter care.

Do septic tank additives replace pumping?

A healthy septic system normally gets the bacteria it needs from everyday household waste. Additives are not a substitute for regular pumping. The U.S. EPA's SepticSmart guidance is a good place to read more about caring for a septic system.

Questions about septic bacteria

What do bacteria do in a septic tank?

Anaerobic bacteria in the tank break down solids. Heavier material settles as sludge, and lighter material, including grease, floats as scum. Wastewater in the middle, called effluent, leaves the tank toward the drain field.

How do bacteria in the drain field treat wastewater?

Aerobic bacteria in the drain-field soil, and a thin living layer called the biomat, treat effluent after it leaves the tank and before it moves farther toward groundwater.

What can kill or overload good septic bacteria?

How does pumping help the drain field?

Regular pumping removes sludge and scum that bacteria cannot fully break down, so solids are less likely to leave the tank and overload the drain field.
